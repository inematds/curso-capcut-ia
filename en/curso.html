Recognize media, preview, and timeline

You can find the areas used to select and adjust a clip.

Changing the wrong object can seem like a program bug. Check what is selected before changing the panel.

In 1 minute

  1. Library: available files
  2. Preview: result at the current point
  3. Timeline: order, layers, and duration

1Three areas and one selection

The library gathers available files.

The preview shows the frame at the current point.

The timeline organizes duration and order.

Drag cena-1 to the timeline.

Click the clip and look at the side panel with video properties.

The properties panel follows the selected item.

This panel may also be called the inspector.

Select text to see text controls; select audio to see sound controls.

Before looking for a button, check what type of item is selected.

Nanda selects the video before changing its scale.

2Layers show what appears in front

Add cena-2 above the first track and align their starts.

The upper image usually covers the lower one.

Reduce its scale in the Video, Basic, or Transform panel, depending on the version.

The lower layer becomes visible around the edges.

Scale changes the size inside the frame; aspect ratio sets the shape of the whole frame.

Do not confuse the two.

After the test, remove the overlay and keep the clips in sequence.

Use Undo if the result is not what you expected.

Jorge uses a short overlay to understand which layer is in front.

3Check the decision against its use

Select the item you want and check the preview after each change.

Jorge uses the checklist to confirm: timeline: order, layers, and duration.

In 1 minute

  2. Sequence: four shots, about 20 seconds
  3. Planned output: 1080 × 1920, 30 frames per second

1Frame and sequence

In the preview’s aspect-ratio area, select 9:16.

Place cena-1, cena-2, cena-3, and cena-4 in sequence.

The kit files are already vertical.

Play the twenty seconds and look for gaps between clips.

The sequence moves from a pot to the materials, then a detail, and ends with the full arrangement.

This is a presentation idea, not a planting demonstration.

If you use your own clips, choose shots that help viewers understand the same invitation.

Nanda accepts losing an empty edge to keep the whole pot in frame.

Cut with purpose and preserve the source

You can split and shorten a clip without deleting the original file.

An impulsive cut can leave a sentence or gesture unfinished. Choose the point before deleting.

In 1 minute

  1. Point: playhead at the desired position
  2. Action: split or trim an edge
  3. Review: transition and sync

1The playhead marks the cut point

Move the playhead to the desired point.

Select the clip and choose Split, usually shown as a blade.

This creates two sections in the edit.

Delete only the unwanted section and play the transition.

Dragging a clip edge shortens its duration on the timeline.

The original source stays in its folder.

To restore a section, extend the edge if media remains, or use Undo.

Do not delete the file from its folder to shorten the edit.

Nanda cuts the section that repeats information.

2Shortcuts come after the gesture

Open your CapCut shortcut list and look for Split and trim left or right of the playhead.

Q and W appear in some shortcut maps.

Check the actual command in your version before using those keys.

The visible button is still a valid option.

After deleting, check whether later clips moved closer automatically.

Track snapping and closing gaps vary by setting.

Check titles and audio too: an edit without gaps can still lose sync.

Jorge plays the transition instead of judging it from thumbnails alone.

3Check the decision against its use

Play the transition and check image, text, and audio after the cut.

Jorge uses the checklist to confirm: review: transition and sync.

Use freeze and reverse with a reason

You can create two short variations and choose one based on its purpose.

A timing effect can draw attention and also confuse the action. Compare before adding it.

In 1 minute

  1. Freeze: pause for a set duration
  2. Reverse: check the direction in playback
  3. Choice: record the effect’s purpose

1Freeze gives viewers time to understand

Select a clip and place the playhead on the desired frame.

Look for Freeze.

The editor creates a still section; adjust its duration by dragging the edge.

Try about one second, but do not treat that as a universal rule.

A pause can give viewers time to read an invitation.

In the kit, the plant already comes from an animated image.

Freeze stops the zoom; it does not reveal real leaf movement.

Check whether the pause feels intentional or like a playback glitch.

Nanda uses a pause only where there is information to read.

2Reverse changes the direction of time

Duplicate a short section and apply Reverse.

Wait for processing to finish.

In the kit, the zoom becomes a pullback.

In filmed footage, liquids, smoke, and gestures may reveal the reversal.

Check the sound too before keeping the version.

If the tool can export a still frame from the preview, save an image for reference.

Check the available format and resolution.

If that command is unavailable, use a screenshot only as a note; it is not a delivery-quality still.

Jorge notices that reversing the kit changes the zoom direction, not the plant’s state.

3Check the decision against its use

Use a timing change when it helps viewers read and does not falsify a demonstration.

Jorge uses the checklist to confirm: choice: record the effect’s purpose.

Test yourself

When should you keep a freeze?

It’s normal to get stuck hereIf the command is unavailable, leave that test pending and continue with the version without the effect.

Control a zoom with two key points

You can create a gradual scale change and check the framing.

A preset zoom may crop the subject or end too soon. Two points let you control the start and end.

In 1 minute

  1. Starting state: scale and point saved
  2. Ending state: slight zoom-in
  3. Review: subject stays in frame

1Two states define the change

A keyframe stores a property value at a specific moment.

Select the clip.

At the start, look for the diamond beside Scale in the Video panel.

Set the current value.

Move the playhead forward and increase the scale slightly.

Check that a second diamond appeared.

Play the interval: the scale should change between the two points.

If everything is large from the start, you probably changed a value without creating the second point.

Undo and check the markers.

Nanda isolates the test in a photo to avoid stacking two movements.

Download the kit with four clips and images · Source material

Four Codex images animated with a zoom. Each clip is five seconds, with no audio; they are not filmed actions or real plant growth.

2Movement needs room

Use a small zoom-in and watch the leaves, pot, and text space.

The kit clips already move; a second zoom may be too much.

For a clean test, use the still image in the kit and extend its duration in the edit.

When available, an easing curve changes the acceleration between points.

Check the simple version first.

Do not add shake, zoom, and animated entry before you know which movement guides attention.

Jorge uses the diamonds as evidence that he saved two states.

3Check the decision against its use

Set two clear states and watch the full interval before adjusting the intensity.

Jorge uses the checklist to confirm: review: subject stays in frame.

Choose among color, removal, and crop

You can compare ways to separate a subject and record their limits.

Cutting out leaves or fine hair can create rough edges. Inspect automatic options.

In 1 minute

  1. Source: plain or varied background
  2. Method: color, automatic removal, or full image
  3. Review: leaves, gaps, shadow, and stability

1Each method depends on the image

Chroma key removes a selected color.

It works best when the background has one color that differs from the subject.

In the video panel, look for Remove background and Chroma key.

Use the eyedropper to select a color and adjust intensity while checking the edges.

The kit background is not uniform, so chroma key is not the first choice.

Automatic removal tries to recognize the subject.

Custom cutout lets you adjust an area, when available.

These features may depend on the version or plan.

Nanda avoids chroma key when the background color also appears in the plant.

2Check it against a contrasting background

To test the edge, place a different color or image on the lower track.

Apply the separation to a short copy.

Check the leaves, gaps, shadow, and pot base.

If important parts disappear, lower the intensity or return to the full image.

Do not approve it from a single frame.

Play the clip and look for edges that flicker.

If the feature is not in your account, use the full image and record that choice.

The goal is to choose a workable method, not buy a feature to finish the lesson.

Jorge prefers the full shot when separation removes leaves.

3Check the decision against its use

Check the edges against a contrasting background; keep the full image if cropping harms the subject.

Jorge uses the checklist to confirm: review: leaves, gaps, shadow, and stability.

Test yourself

Green leaves disappear along with a green background. Which explanation makes sense?

It’s normal to get stuck hereKeeping the full image is a valid choice in this lesson. Do not mark removal as done if it did not happen.
